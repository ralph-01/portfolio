Problem Statement

Managing money across multiple income sources without a centralised view creates blind spots that are hard to identify until damage is done. Most individuals track finances reactively, checking balances after spending rather than understanding the patterns that drive it.

This project was built to demonstrate how MS Excel can move personal finance management beyond simple budgeting apps. It creates a full analytical dashboard that tracks income streams, categorises spending, reveals weekly and monthly patterns, and gives a complete, real-time financial picture across a 10-month period.

Objective
Build an elegant, banking-inspired MS Excel dashboard that consolidates multi-source income and spending into a single view.
Track and compare income vs. spending on both monthly and weekly timelines to surface behavioural patterns.
Break down spending by category to identify the biggest budget drivers and areas for optimisation.
Demonstrate that data tools traditionally used by corporations can be powerfully applied to personal financial management.
Tools Used
MS Excel, with a dark-themed personal finance dashboard featuring a banking-app aesthetic
DAX, for balance calculations, income source percentages, savings rate, and spending ratios
Sparkline trend cards, a donut chart, and a custom MasterCard-style balance visual
Monthly and weekly trend bar charts with dual debit/credit series and month-filter slicers.
Step-by-Step Approach
Step 1
Data collection & structuring
Compiled 10 months of personal financial data covering income from 3 streams and expenses across 5 categories. Organised into a clean transaction table with date, type, category, and amount fields.
Step 2
DAX measures & calculations
Built measures for available balance, total income, total spending, savings rate, income source share percentages, and monthly/weekly debit-credit aggregations.
Step 3
Balance & income visuals
Designed the MasterCard-style available balance card ($35,249), income and spending sparkline KPI cards, and the income source donut chart with three-stream breakdown.
Step 4
Trend & spending analysis
Built the Top Spendings panel, monthly income vs. spending trend bars (Jan–Oct), weekly spending trend chart by day, and month-filter slicer for granular drill-down.

Key Insights
Savings health
A net savings rate of 54%, spending $30.2K against $65.4K in income, reflects a strong and healthy financial position well above the recommended 20% benchmark.
Spending concentration
Housing alone consumes 29.8% of total spending. Combined with groceries, the top two categories account for over 51% of all expenditure.
Digital income growth
YouTube and Teachable together contribute 24% of total income ($15,440). Both are scalable, passive-leaning streams that warrant further investment of time and content.
Weekly spending pattern
Monday is consistently the highest-spend day of the week, an actionable behavioural insight for anyone looking to introduce spending discipline or weekly budget boundaries.
Recommendations
1
Set a conscious Monday spending limit
Since Monday is the highest-spend day, introducing a self-imposed daily budget cap on Mondays, or a 24-hour purchase review rule, could meaningfully reduce total monthly spending.
2
Scale the digital income streams
YouTube and Teachable currently contribute 24% of income with relatively low marginal cost. Increasing content output or course offerings on these platforms could grow their share significantly.
3
Review housing cost strategy
At $9,000, nearly 30% of total spending, housing is the single largest budget line. Exploring options to reduce, offset, or restructure this cost could free up several thousand dollars annually.
4
Investigate the $900 peak spending anomaly
The monthly trend chart reveals a single $900 maximum spend event. Understanding whether this was a one-time purchase or a recurring pattern is important for accurate future budgeting.
Conclusion

This project proves that the analytical power behind enterprise dashboards belongs in everyone's hands, not just corporate finance teams. By applying MS Excel to personal financial data, this dashboard transforms scattered transactions into a clear story of income, spending, and savings. The result is not just a pretty report. It is a financial awareness tool that gives its user the visibility to make smarter, more intentional decisions with their money every single day.
