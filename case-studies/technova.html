Problem Statement

TECHNOVA Gadgets Ltd. is a consumer electronics company with a distributed sales team of 10 representatives operating across five major Indian cities: Mumbai, Hyderabad, Delhi, Bangalore, and Cochin. The company sells three product categories: Laptops, Headphones, and Phones.

Sales managers were working from disconnected spreadsheets with no unified view of rep performance, product category contribution, or city-level revenue trends. Decision-making was slow, and high-performing patterns within the team were going unrecognised and unreplicated.

Objective
Build a single-page executive Power BI dashboard consolidating all sales performance dimensions into one interactive view.
Compare individual sales rep contributions to identify top performers and coaching opportunities.
Break down revenue by city and product category to guide regional and portfolio strategy.
Reveal seasonal revenue patterns across a full 12-month cycle to enable proactive planning.
Tools Used
Power BI Desktop, for executive single-page dashboard layout and design
DAX, for revenue aggregations, per-rep calculations, and category contribution measures
City and month slicers, for dynamic filtering by region and time period
Donut charts, ranked bar charts, KPI card strips, and a 12-month line trend chart
Step-by-Step Approach
Step 1
Data collection & cleaning
Gathered full-year transactional sales data across 10 reps, 5 cities, and 3 product categories. Standardised rep names, city labels, and product classifications for consistent analysis.
Step 2
Data modelling & measures
Built DAX measures for total revenue, revenue per rep, average transaction value, and category share percentages. Created a date table to power the 12-month trend analysis.
Step 3
KPI & performance visuals
Designed the top KPI strip (total revenue, units, rep size, revenue per rep, average revenue, active weeks) and the Sales Rep Performance ranked bar chart with individual revenue totals.
Step 4
Revenue & category analysis
Built Revenue by City horizontal bars, Revenue Performance Over Time line chart, Top Selling Products ranking, Category Contribution donut chart, and Sales Volume by Rep visual.

Key Insights
Product strategy
Laptops drive 41.4% of revenue, making them the strategic anchor. Pricing and promotions in this category have an outsized effect on total revenue performance.
Team performance
Amar leads at $1.14M, 35% above the team average of $846K. His approach represents a replicable playbook for the rest of the team.
City-level opportunity
Mumbai and Hyderabad lead at ~$1.75M each. Cochin, despite being the smallest market at $1.55M, shows strong potential for targeted growth investment.
Seasonal pattern
A recurring mid-year revenue dip is visible in the 12-month trend, a predictable pattern that proactive campaigns and incentives can counteract before it happens.
Recommendations
1
Document and share Amar's sales approach
Analyse the products, cities, and timing patterns behind Amar's $1.14M performance and build a shared best-practice guide for the wider team to close the performance gap.
2
Invest in Cochin as a growth market
With $1.55M in revenue from the smallest represented city, Cochin has disproportionate ROI potential. A targeted rep assignment or regional campaign could unlock significant upside.
3
Address the mid-year dip with planned campaigns
The 12-month trend confirms a predictable Q2–Q3 slowdown. Pre-emptive mid-year promotions, new product launches, or team incentive programmes should be built into the annual calendar.
4
Revisit the Phones category pricing strategy
Phones generate only 19.7% of revenue despite being a high-transaction category. A margin and pricing review could significantly improve this category's contribution to overall revenue.
Conclusion

This project shows how a single, well-designed Power BI dashboard can replace a stack of disconnected spreadsheets and bring clarity to every layer of a sales operation, from individual rep rankings to city-level revenue distribution and category strategy. The TECHNOVA dashboard does not just report what happened; it surfaces the patterns, gaps, and opportunities that turn a good sales team into a great one.
