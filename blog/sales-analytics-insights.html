5 Sales Analytics Insights That Most Businesses Miss — And How to Find Them

6 min read May 2025 Ogunleye R.A.
RO
Ogunleye R.A.
Data Analyst, MS Excel, Power BI, Data Visualisation

Most sales teams track the obvious metrics: total revenue, units sold, and maybe top-performing reps. But the data that drives the biggest strategic decisions often lives beneath the surface. Here are five sales analytics insights that organisations consistently overlook, and exactly where to find them in your own dashboards.

1
Which day of the week is your most profitable?

Most businesses think in months and quarters. Very few think in weekdays. Yet when you break down profit by day of the week, patterns emerge that can fundamentally change how you allocate your sales team's effort.

In the GloMark Field Sales Analysis, Wednesday and Thursday contributed 43.8% of total profit, a powerful finding that could drive decisions around when to schedule key client calls, send proposals, or run promotions. Weekday transactions overall drove 72% of all profit, meaning weekends are a significantly lower-yield window despite consuming equal calendar time.

MS Excel tip: Build a weekday breakdown using a calculated column, =TEXT(date_cell,"ddd"), and map it to a PivotChart sorted by day. The pattern it reveals may surprise your entire sales team.

2
The 80/20 rule is probably hiding in your product data

The Pareto Principle, the idea that 20% of inputs drive 80% of outputs, is almost always true in product sales. But most companies have never actually visualised it. In the GloMark project, just five product variants from the Mountain-200 series contributed 24.8% of all profit, while 448 out of 606 available products remained completely unsold.

A product profitability matrix in MS Excel can reveal exactly which items to prioritise, which to discount aggressively, and which to discontinue entirely. The GloMark data also revealed that products priced above $150 generate 95.4% of all profit, a pricing insight that fundamentally shapes the entire product and marketing strategy.

MS Excel tip: Create a scatter chart with unit price on the X axis and profit contribution on the Y axis. Products clustering in the top-right are your Pareto winners; those are the ones deserving the most sales attention and inventory investment.

3
Geography tells you where to grow next

Revenue by city or country is a standard metric. But when you overlay it with profit margin by location, the picture changes dramatically. A city generating high revenue with thin margins needs a very different strategy than a smaller market generating high-margin transactions.

In the TECHNOVA dashboard, Cochin generated $1.55M in revenue, the lowest of the five cities, yet showed strong potential for disproportionate ROI with targeted investment. Meanwhile in GloMark, the US and Australia together drive 62.7% of global profit from just two of six markets, raising an important question: are the other four markets underperforming because of market conditions or because of underinvestment?

Power BI tip: Use a filled map visual with profit margin as the colour saturation measure. High-revenue, low-colour cities immediately flag where your pricing or cost structure needs attention.

4
The mid-year revenue dip is almost universal and preventable

Look at any full-year revenue line chart and you will almost certainly see a dip somewhere between April and August. TECHNOVA's 12-month trend chart confirmed this pattern clearly: a valley in the middle of the year followed by a strong second-half recovery.

Businesses that anticipate this dip and plan mid-year campaigns, seasonal promotions, or new product launches around it consistently outperform competitors who react to the dip after it has already happened. The data does not just show you the past. It lets you plan around the future.

Power BI tip: Plot a 12-month revenue line alongside the prior year as a reference line. Where the curves consistently dip in the same months, you have a confirmed seasonal pattern, and a clear window to place proactive campaigns.

5
Your top rep's success is replicable

In every sales team, there are one or two reps significantly outperforming the rest. Most businesses know who they are. Fewer businesses have actually analysed what they are doing differently, and fewer still have built that knowledge into a system the rest of the team can follow.

In TECHNOVA's dashboard, Amar generated $1.14M, 35% above the team average of $846K and nearly double the revenue of the lowest-performing reps. Power BI can help correlate rep performance with the products they focus on, the cities they cover, their transaction frequency, and the time of year they close most deals. That pattern, once visible, is a playbook waiting to be documented and shared.

Power BI tip: Build a rep performance matrix with revenue, units, average deal size, and city concentration as columns. Sort by revenue and look for patterns in the rows above the median; those are the behaviours worth replicating across the whole team.

The businesses that win are not the ones with the most data; they are the ones who read it fastest and act with confidence. These five insights are not hidden because the data is hard to find. They are hidden because most teams never look past the headline numbers. The right tool just makes looking easy.

Every one of these insights came directly from real dashboard projects: GloMark's weekday profit patterns, TECHNOVA's rep performance gaps, and product profitability analysis across thousands of transactions. The tools to surface them are available to any business today. The only question is whether you are using them.
