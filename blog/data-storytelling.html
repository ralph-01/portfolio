Data Storytelling — Why the Best Dashboards Speak to People, Not Just Analysts

4 min read May 2025 Ogunleye R.A.
RO
Ogunleye R.A.
Data Analyst — MS Excel, Power BI, Data Visualisation

There is a critical difference between a dashboard that displays data and a dashboard that tells a story. One answers the question "What are the numbers?" The other answers "What do the numbers mean, and what should we do about them?" The best dashboards sit firmly in the second category. And the difference comes down to design thinking, not just data skill.

The insight-first principle

Every dashboard should be designed around the decisions it needs to support, not around the data that happens to be available. Before building a single visual, the best analysts ask: "What question is this dashboard answering? Who will read it? What action do I want them to take?"

This insight-first approach is what separates a reporting tool from a decision-making engine. Data without a question is just noise. A dashboard without a decision to support is just decoration.

Before building a single visual, write down the single most important question your dashboard needs to answer. Every element you add should either answer that question directly or provide context that helps the reader understand the answer better.

The three principles of a compelling data story

①
Principle 1
Context — numbers without meaning are just noise

A standalone number means nothing. "$307 million in revenue" is impressive, but only when placed alongside cost of goods ($180M), profit ($126M), and margin (41.1%) does it tell a story worth acting on.

The GloMark dashboard solved this by immediately surrounding every headline number with its natural context, showing not just what was earned, but what it cost, what remained, and how efficiently it was generated. Every KPI earned its place by making its neighbours more meaningful.

②
Principle 2
Hierarchy — guide the eye, don't overwhelm it

Not all data is equally important. A great dashboard guides the reader's eye: the most critical KPIs sit at the top, with supporting detail available on demand through filters and drill-downs. This mirrors how humans naturally process information: headline first, detail second.

③
Principle 3
Interactivity — answer questions you didn't know to ask

Static reports answer the question you thought to ask. Interactive dashboards answer the question you did not know you had.

When a sales manager filters the GloMark dashboard by country and immediately sees how profit margin, product mix, and weekday patterns shift in that specific context, the dashboard has just generated an insight that no static monthly report could ever have surfaced. That is the true power of interactivity: it turns passive reading into active discovery.

Design is not decoration

Dark themes, carefully chosen colour palettes, icon-based KPI cards, and consistent typography are not cosmetic choices. They reduce cognitive load, increase readability, and make it easier for non-technical stakeholders to absorb and trust what they are seeing.

The Personal Finance Tracker's banking-app aesthetic was entirely deliberate, and it made financial data feel familiar and approachable rather than intimidating. The TECHNOVA dashboard's clean white layout was designed for executive presentations where clarity matters more than complexity. Every design choice communicates something to the reader before they read a single number.

Data storytelling in practice — three real examples

🌍
GloMark
The story: "Q2 and mid-week are your profit window. Plan everything around them." Told through quarterly bars and a weekday breakdown that made the pattern impossible to miss.
💻
TECHNOVA
The story: "One rep is 35% above average. Find out why and teach it to everyone." Told through a ranked bar chart that made the performance gap immediately visible.
💳
Finance Tracker
The story: "You save 54% of your income, but Monday is quietly draining your budget." Told through a savings ring and a weekly spend chart in a single scroll.

In data analytics, your job is not finished when the data is correct. It is finished when the person reading the dashboard understands what to do next. That is the true power of data storytelling, and it is the standard every dashboard should be held to.

The most powerful dashboards are not built by the analysts who know the most about data. They are built by analysts who understand people: how they think, what they need to decide, and what it takes to move them to action. Master the three principles of context, hierarchy, and interactivity, and your dashboards will stop being reports and start being conversations.
